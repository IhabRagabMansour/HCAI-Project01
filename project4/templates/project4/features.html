Movie Feature Representation

Every movie is encoded as a vector x ∈ R{{ dim }}, and a participant's taste as a latent vector w of the same size, with utility U(x) = wTx. Because w must be estimated from only a handful of interactions, the representation is deliberately small, dense, and human-meaningful — every dimension is something a person could plausibly have a taste about.

Excluded Features

imdb_score, gross, num_voted_users and the Facebook-like counts are deliberately excluded: they measure a film's fame or critical acclaim rather than the participant's intrinsic taste, and including them would let w absorb a popularity prior that merely looks like a preference. language is also dropped — 93% of the corpus is English, so the feature is nearly constant and carries almost no signal.

Preprocessing Decisions

  • Missing values: movies without genres, duration, or release year are dropped; missing content rating and country become Other.
  • Multi-valued genres: the pipe-separated field becomes a multi-hot indicator over the corpus genre vocabulary.
  • Rare categories: the 16 raw content ratings collapse into four audience-maturity buckets, avoiding one-hot dimensions with only a few movies.
  • Numerical scaling: duration and release year are standardized using corpus statistics stored in the encoder, so the transform is reproducible.
  • Binary features stay 0/1: genre and country indicators are not standardized, so each coefficient of w reads directly as “how much this genre is liked”.

The same encoder and the same matrix X are used by both elicitation conditions, so neither interface is advantaged by its representation.
